# CMS Transmittal 166 — read at source

**CMS Manual System, Transmittal 166. Change Request 3751.**
Subject: **Overnight Oximetry Testing.** Dated 22 July 2005.
Effective and implementation dates: 22 August 2005.
`https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/transmittals/downloads/r166otn.pdf`
Retrieved 19 September 2026.

## What it establishes

The home overnight oximetry structure — beneficiary self-administration under
IDTF direction, the sealed tamper-proof unit, the DME supplier permitted to
deliver and transmit but not to instruct or touch the results — **dates to 2005**.
Not to the 2019 or 2020 revisions. It is twenty-one years old.

It specifies HCPCS 94762 and requires that "the test unit is sealed and
tamper-proof such that test results cannot be accessed by anyone other than the
IDTF."

## Why this closes something

The silence is not an oversight in a recent revision. The instruction that
created unsupervised home oximetry for Medicare oxygen qualification specified
custody, chain of handling, and who may speak to the beneficiary — and specified
nothing whatever about the instrument that produces the number.

Twenty-one years, and the parameter that determines whether a desaturation is
recorded at all has never appeared in the instruction, the LCD, or the NCD.

This is the third independent source confirming the same absence:
Transmittal 166 (2005), L33797 R11 (2023), NCD 240.2 v2 (2021).
